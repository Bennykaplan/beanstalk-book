## Comparison

| | Real people (10) | AI reviewers (10) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Looks like me | 7 yes, 1 partly, 2 no | Average 2.9 of 5 |
| Story rings true | 9 yes, 1 didn't understand the question | Average 3.4 of 5 |
| Looks professional | 9 yes (several "I guess"), 1 didn't understand the question | Average 3.7 of 5 |
| Named a real problem | 3 of 10 | 10 of 10 |

The real people answered yes or no, so those counts are my reading of their words. They aren't on the same scale as the AI scores.

**Both caught:** the character looks too young, and the likeness could be closer. This is the strongest finding, because the two groups found it separately. Person 1 said "me as a 5 year old", Person 5 asked for "older and not like a 5 year old", and AI reviewers said much the same.

**People caught, and the AI didn't:**
- **The wait.** Person 1 said it took a while to load. The AI reviewers only saw finished pages and never used the app, so they couldn't notice.
- **Confusing survey questions.** Person 6 didn't know what "professional" meant here, and Person 9 asked what "ring true" meant. I should have explained those questions. The AI answered every question without hesitating.
- **Whether it's good enough.** Most people simply accepted it ("good enough", "all good"). The AI found fault everywhere, so it can't tell me what an ordinary person would actually put up with.
- **The real verdict on likeness.** Only the actual person can say whether a face is theirs. Two people said no outright, and the AI can only compare pictures.

**The AI caught, and people didn't:**
- About Me showed the same figure twice, which looked like a mistake.
- The child's name is on About Me, but the story says Jack.
- Continuity: about nine beans on page 3 against five on page 2, and the mother's apron changing between pages.
- Page 2's text crowding the characters, and page 3 splitting attention between two focal points.
- Accessibility for weak readers, and contrast.
- Faces moving toward a generic cartoon look, including one reviewer saying Asian features were softened.

**How far to trust each group:**
- **The real responses are short and probably kind.** They came from friends and family, who tend to be polite, and most gave one-word answers. Only 3 of 10 named a real problem.
- **Some AI complaints are unverified, and some are invented.** In an earlier AI run, one reviewer complained the cow was missing from page 3, where it doesn't belong. AI also tends to find a problem because it was asked to. I didn't check every claim against the pictures.
- **They saw different books.** Each person reviewed a book of their own face. The AI reviewed three test books (one of them from a photo of me, two from AI-generated faces), made before the changes below.

**Takeaway:** people told me what matters most (does it look like me, and how old), and AI gave me a long, cheap list of details to check. I'd use AI to find defects before showing people, and people to decide which defects matter.

## What I changed because of the feedback

| Feedback | Change | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Looks too young (people and AI) | Prompt now says never to make the person younger, and that adults stay adults | Better for adults, but they now look like short adults. Children still look a little young. **Not solved** |
| About Me shows the figure twice (AI) | PDF crops to one figure | Fixed |
| Name vs. Jack (AI) | About Me now says "starring as Jack" | Better. The story text itself still says Jack, as the storyboard requires |
| Too many beans, apron changes (AI) | Prompt now says exactly five beans and a fixed apron | Looks better in two test books, not proven |
| Slow to load (Person 1) | Page now says how long it takes, and each image has its own progress bar | The first visit is still slow because the free server has to wake up |

## 10 AI responses

Each reviewer looked at the whole PDF of one of three test books. Scores are 1 to 5.

### AI reviewer 1

- **Does this look like me? (2/5)** The child on pages 1-3 and the About Me page has the same short brown hair and a headband like my daughter, but the face doesn’t really match the photo. In the book she has huge round eyes, a tiny button nose, and a much more babyish face than the real picture.
- **Does the story ring true? (2/5)** What decides it for me is whether she’d feel like this is really her, and page 2 already loses me because the text says 'old man' but the picture makes it look like she’s happily trading the cow with no worry at all. On page 3 the line says 'Beans! she cried,' but the child is standing there smiling calmly while the mother throws them out the window, so the feeling on the page doesn’t match the words.
- **Does it look professional? (3/5)** Pages 1-3 are consistent in color and character design, and the text is easy to read in white against the darker parts of the picture. But the About Me page looks tacked on compared with the story pages, and having two near-identical full-body images side by side on that page makes it feel more like a draft than a finished book.
- **What isn't good?** I wouldn’t pay for this today. The main problem is the girl doesn’t look enough like my daughter across every page, especially the face on pages 1-3 and the About Me page; the hair is close, but the features are generic cartoon-child features instead of hers.
- **Also noticed:** Page 1 and page 3 make the child look much younger than 8. On page 1 especially, next to the mother and table, the character reads more like a preschooler in size and proportions, so the age on the About Me page doesn’t match the story art.

### AI reviewer 5

- **Does this look like me? (2/5)** The character keeps the black glasses and dark hair from the uploaded photo, but the face is turned into a very young child with a tiny nose, round cheeks, huge eyes, and no resemblance to the adult man's actual facial structure. On the About Me page, the child model looks more like a generic cartoon boy than this specific person.
- **Does the story ring true? (3/5)** The sequence reads clearly: first Jack and his mother with the cow, then the old man offering beans, then the mother throwing them out the window. The problem is age logic: the personalized character looks about five while the About Me page says age 9, and the uploaded photo is an adult man, so the personalization fights the story setup.
- **Does it look professional? (2/5)** Page 2 is the page I would send back. The line of type is shoved across the top over sky and heads, and the second sentence starts in the same line, so the reading path is muddy. Across all pages, the character consistency is only partial: glasses and outfit repeat, but the face is a generic toddler version rather than a stable likeness of the uploaded person.
- **Also noticed:** Page 3 has a directional logic problem in the picture itself: the mother is reaching out from the left window and the beans are falling in a vertical arc below that window, but the child is staged separately in the doorway on the right, looking forward rather than toward the action. The page splits into two disconnected focal points instead of one readable action beat.
